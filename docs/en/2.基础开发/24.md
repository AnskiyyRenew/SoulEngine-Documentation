# Camera

During gameplay, the camera is not always fixed. It can pan, rotate, or even zoom. This page explains all of those operations.

---

## Direct camera control

Use `camera:setPosition(x, y)` to move the camera to position `(x, y)`.

Use `camera:setScale(x, y)` to scale the camera horizontally and vertically.

Use `camera:setAngle(r)` to rotate the camera.

```lua
_CAMERA_:setPosition(320, 240)
_CAMERA_:setScale(0.5, 0.5)
_CAMERA_:setAngle(math.rad(45))
```

---

## Boundary constraints

In Overworld, the camera is constrained inside a specific region when the map shrinks. Inside the region, the camera follows the player; outside the region, it stays fixed. This ensures smooth room transitions.

Here are two ways to define boundaries:

1. Direct coordinates: `camera:setBounds(0, 0, 800, 600)` constrains the camera to the area from `(0,0)` to `(800,600)`.

2. Block rectangle: `camera:setBoundsBlock(100, 100, 400, 300)` constrains the camera to a rectangle with top-left corner `(100,100)` and size `400×300`.

*Note: enable boundary detection with `camera.boundActive = true` (default is off).*

## Screen shake

When firing a cannon or when the blue soul hits the ground, the screen may shake slightly.

Use `camera:shake(magnitude, duration, decay)` to set up camera shake.

- `magnitude`: shake strength in pixels.
- `duration`: how many frames the shake lasts.
- `decay`: defaults to true. If true, the shake strength decreases over time. If false, it remains constant.

```lua
camera:shake(10, 60, true)   -- heavy shake with 10px amplitude for 60 frames, fading out
camera:shake(5, 30, false)   -- light shake with 5px amplitude for 30 frames, no decay
```
